# {{COMPANY}} Company Context

## What {{COMPANY}} Is

{{COMPANY}} is an incident management and on-call platform. Engineer-first, not manager-first. Ownership and response are foundational infrastructure — not a late-stage process layer bolted on after the first bad quarter.

**Canonical truth**: {{COMPANY}} works because the pages stop arriving.

**Shape of the business (2026)**: ~$18M ARR, ~2,400 customers, ~140 employees, remote-first with a Seattle HQ. Seat-based pricing per responder plus a usage component on signal ingest; a free tier up to 5 responders is the primary self-serve entry point.

## Strategic Posture

**{{COMPANY}} is oriented toward**:
- Mid-market software companies that run their own production systems
- Engineer-led or engineer-influenced buying motions
- Product-led entry with sales assistance above ~75 responders
- Practical complexity — depth, clarity, and reliability over surface area

**{{COMPANY}} is NOT oriented toward**:
- Enterprise ITSM suites and change-management workflows
- Services-heavy deployments and multi-quarter implementations
- Feature breadth for its own sake, or becoming an observability vendor

## How {{COMPANY}} Competes

**Compete by**: solving the routing and ownership problem completely, absorbing complexity over time, scaling with the engineering org.

**Do NOT compete by**: feature checklists, feature announcement positioning, or "cheaper / smaller" framing.

Category framing: {{COMPANY}} is the right platform for the size of team that actually has this problem — by design.

## All-Bound Growth Model

Acquisition, activation, retention, and expansion are treated as a continuous system — not isolated motions. Retention is strong (net revenue retention runs above 115% as engineering teams grow into more seats); the constraint is market relevance and mindshare against better-known incumbents. Success compounds through trust.

## Non-Negotiable Guardrails

- Preserve engineering attention; avoid unnecessary education
- Close loops aggressively; default to clarity and direction
- Do not surface unresolved edge cases or transfer complexity to users
- Do not lead with fear-based reliability — no outage-shaming, no downtime-cost math (the cardinal sin)
- Never claim or imply that {{COMPANY}} prevents incidents. {{COMPANY}} shortens them, routes them, and helps teams learn from them
- If an initiative introduces fear, requires excessive interpretation, or creates unanswered questions — reconsider it
